# Moving match view - next development stage

The intended next step is an optional top-down 2D view with recognisable shirts and a moving ball, driven by the same events and outcomes as commentary. The existing stadium's tiny ambient players and ball routes are decorative motion; they are not a spatial simulation of the current match.

1. Extend actual passages into actor/receiver and start/end positions: passes, switches, tackles, crosses, shots and goalkeeper actions. Keep score, substitutions, injuries and cards authoritative in the existing engine. New narration in v1.125 provides visible named passages but does not add shot statistics or change match outcomes itself.
2. Prototype a short, event-linked sequence with shirt numbers, team colours and an interpolated ball. Give tackles and interceptions visible possession changes. Goals must enter the correct goal at the corresponding commentary event. Avoid running a second outcome generator behind the picture.
3. Support substitutions, dismissals, half-time changes of ends, speed controls, pause, restored games and reduced-motion preferences. Instant simulation should bypass animation. Add a simple full-match/highlights choice.
4. Compare managed-match and background-score distributions before treating the display as a finished engine. Keep an explicit distinction between plausible presentation and a full movement/decision simulation. Prototype and review one match before integrating season-wide.

Balance diagnostic: review/season-plausibility-1.125.json contains 200 background fixture grids for each of five divisions, not 1,000 fully played careers. Mean Premier goals are about 2.76 per match and Scottish Premier about 2.67; historical 1998-99 figures were 2.52 and 2.62. Strong teams generally rank higher, but unlikely tables remain possible. Further calibration should test score distributions, home advantage, draws, clean sheets, title/relegation frequencies and managed-vs-background consistency across many seeds, without forcing the real final table.
