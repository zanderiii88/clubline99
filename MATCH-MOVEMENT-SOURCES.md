Consulted 17 November 2026. These primary-authored period reports provide context, not measured constants or club-specific movement data.

- FIFA, France 98 Technical Report, technique/tactics section around pp. 28–30. Consulted via an OCR transcription hosted at https://doczz.net/doc/2687170/france-1998-part-1 . It discusses advancing wide defenders, covering midfielders and counterattacking space. OCR wording requires care.

Implementation thresholds, travel speeds and decision rules are design choices, tested in the lab; they are not values specified by these reports. No report text, images or PDFs are bundled. Further primary footage/club-level analysis can refine the model later.

## v1.140: elapsed versus effective playing time

Meyer, Ohlendorf and Kindermann (2000), *Konditionelle Fähigkeiten deutscher Spitzenfußballer im Längsschnitt*, Deutsche Zeitschrift für Sportmedizin 51(7–8), pp.271–277. Publisher's primary article: https://www.germanjournalsportsmedicine.com/fileadmin/content/archiv2000/heft07_08/a07_07800.pdf (accessed 17 November 2026). The authors measured effective time in selected Bundesliga matches through the first half of 1997/98 and distinguished it from elapsed duration; their sample selected six meetings among the final top three teams each season. The World Cup figures in the article are attributed to FIFA reports, not their own measurements. This is useful period context, not a representative English or Scottish benchmark.

The lab's eight restart-delay ranges are explicit prototype design values, not values measured by that paper. They represent retrieval and setup for actual stoppages. No extra stoppages, effective-time quotas, forced outcomes or score corrections are introduced to match a benchmark. Remaining time-in-play, foul/penalty, shot and pass rates still require calibration against appropriate historical competition data. Defensive shot-lane reach and tackle-recovery intervals are also implementation hypotheses, not historical measurements.

## Through balls and one-twos

- FIFA Training Centre, [Dennis Hediger's passing patterns for balls behind the defence](https://www.fifatrainingcentre.com/en/practice/elite-sessions/in-possession/hediger-balls-behind-the-defence.php). The exercises pair a weighted pass with the timing of a runner into the space behind the back line. The lab checks both the route of the pass and the space available when the runner gets there.
- FIFA Training Centre, [Wall passes in tight spaces](https://www.fifatrainingcentre.com/en/practice/talent-coach-programme/create-and-finish/1v1plus-2-small-sided-game-wall-passes-in-tight-spaces.php). The move starts when a defender steps out. The carrier plays to a nearby teammate, moves past the defender and looks for the return. The lab reassesses the lane and offside at the return pass.

These coaching examples inform the shape and timing of the moves. Their publication dates and exercise results supply no late-1990s match-frequency estimates.
