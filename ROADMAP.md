## Agreed match-engine sequence
1. Phone-test v1.136 build-up and existing keeper/cover/delivery tests. Review tempo, pressure decisions, safe back passes/switches, screens and heading choices. Continue full-session chance/pass-volume calibration and skill/tactical comparisons. In ninety three-minute strength controls, stronger sides score 34–0 with 238–4 shots across sixty mismatches (24 wins, 36 draws, no losses); equal-tier results are Blue 4 wins, Red 3, 23 draws. Short samples do not establish season/full-match realism. Do not force scores or passing totals.
2. Consolidate post-v1.139 balance: set-piece/score/chance/pass volume and injury rates across broader seeds and skill/tactical setups. Review manager injury decisions and replacement choices on phones. Continue broader foul/keeper/handball offences, persistent fouling and delayed cautions. Injuries are still match-only prototype consequences, without diagnoses, recovery weeks or career medical effects. Research each competition's nominated bench and disciplinary rules before career integration; five named substitutes are the current lab fixture, not a universal competition claim.
3. Match presentation and time: one incremental underlying simulation/event ledger; Key Highlights (mobile default), Extended Highlights and Full Match. Readable action with modest playback speed; fewer passages for quicker completion. Between passages, advance the clock and update compact stats, text incidents and evidence-based tactical observations. Pause for tactics and important decisions; changes affect only future simulation. Do not precompute an unchangeable final result or invent stats to hit targets. Validate viewing-mode/seed equivalence and intervention behaviour.
4. Better defensive organisation, shot selection by angle/pressure/keeper position, passes/first touches, transition roles, tiredness and more complete aerial contests/animations. Validate scoring/chance frequencies and strength gaps across longer sessions; three-minute results cannot be extrapolated into 90-minute or season realism.
5. Referee judgement and media: occasional genuinely marginal offside decisions, with possible errors and no reversal. Build interviews from actual consequential match incidents: disagree, agree, did not see, move on. Responses can affect squad/supporter sentiment, disciplinary attention and manager rivalries. Do not invent controversies or imply the manager has perfect knowledge. Existing career media tools are separate from this future spatial-match link.

## Permanent historical constraint
No VAR in CLUBLINE 99, in any season or mode: no review flow, video-check event, VAR text, replay adjudication or later overturning of decisions. The referee and assistants make the on-field decisions. Visual highlights can show play; they never become an officiating review.
